How it works

Work in layers. Bill on one page.

You already think this way. A project file is a stack of layers, and so is the money you are owed for it.

  1. 01

    Log the work as layers

    Each kind of work gets its own layer: studio hours, revision rounds, licences, expenses. Log them as the job runs, from the timer or by hand, so nothing has to be rebuilt from your calendar in week six.

  2. 02

    Flatten when the job ships

    Pick the layers that belong on this invoice. Tally merges them into line items, subtracts the deposit, works out the tax and numbers the page in sequence.

  3. 03

    Send it, and let Tally chase

    The client gets a link and a PDF. If the due date passes, Tally sends a polite reminder on day 1, day 7 and day 14. You do not have to write the awkward email.

Built for design work

The lines other invoicing tools leave out.

Revision rounds
Say how many rounds the quote includes. The round after that becomes its own line at your hourly rate, with the date the client asked for it.
Licence and usage
Territory, term and media sit on the invoice as a priced line, not in an email thread nobody can find a year later.
Deposits
Bill half before you open a file. The final invoice subtracts it.
Kill fees
When a job is cancelled, Tally flattens the work done so far and adds the fee from your contract.
Your own page
Set the invoice in your typeface, with your mark and your grid. It is the last thing the client sees from you, so it should look like you made it.
