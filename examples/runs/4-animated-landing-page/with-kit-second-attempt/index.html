One invoice, three sheets

A paper invoice pad writes every invoice three times at once, through the carbon. Tally works the same way, so you never retype a number or wonder which version the client saw.

  1. Top copy goes to the client

    A clean page with your logo, the job, and a way to pay. Clients pay by card or bank transfer without creating an account.

  2. Carbon copy stays with you

    An exact duplicate, locked the moment you send. If a client disputes the third revision round six months later, you have the page they agreed to, with the date they opened it. You can correct an invoice, but the correction is a new sheet. The old one stays in the pad.

  3. Ledger sheet goes to your books

    Every invoice posts to a running ledger: what is owed, what is late, what has been paid. Export it as a CSV for your accountant at the end of the quarter.

The small print, in your favour

Design work has terms that a generic invoice template leaves out. Tally prints them on the sheet, so the awkward conversation happens before the job and not after.

  1. Deposits

    Ask for 30% or 50% before you open a file. Tally takes it off the final invoice and shows the client the sum.

  2. Revision rounds

    Set how many rounds the fee includes. Round three and beyond become a line on the invoice, priced in advance. The client sees the count on every proof you send, so the extra line is never a surprise.

  3. Usage licences

    Bill the licence apart from the labour: exclusive or not, for how long, in which media. The terms print under the line.

  4. Late fees

    Net 14 means 14. On day 15 Tally adds the fee you set and sends a reminder in words you wrote, not a collection agency's.

  5. Kill fees

    If the project is cancelled, bill the work done to date at the percentage in your contract.
