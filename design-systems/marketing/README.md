# Marketing

Pick this for landing pages, product pages, pricing pages and launch announcements: pages read once, quickly, by someone deciding whether to care.

Do not pick it for the product itself. If the page needs 3D or a signature animation, pick `showcase`.

## What went wrong with the first version

The first version of this variant was all restraint: system font, one colour, no depth. I ran it against an agent with no guidance and the unguided page looked better. Mine was tidy and had no pulse. Rules that only take things away produce a page nobody minds and nobody remembers.

This version keeps the discipline and asks for presence.

## What changes from base

**A concept.** One sentence from the product's own world, written before anything else. It picks the typeface, the colours, the motif and the tone of the words.

**Type with character.** `font.pairings` lists four open-licence pairs. Pick by concept, or bring another open pair and say why. Never the system font on a landing page. `font.label` is a monospace face for small labels, the kind printed on forms and packaging.

**A loud headline.** Display is 80px (44px on small screens), weight 800, tracking pulled tight. It is used once, runs to three lines at most, and is the largest thing on the page by a wide margin.

**Two colours.** An accent for action, with its three jobs. A support colour for mood: a highlight behind one phrase of the headline, a sheet behind the product, a band across one section. Three uses at most. Choose both from the concept. The defaults are burnt orange and sage.

**The product has presence.** The hero shows the real product, at least 45% of the width, with the `product` shadow or sitting on a sheet of the support colour with the `hard` shadow. A flat grey box with a border is not presence.

**One motif.** Something small that repeats and belongs to the concept: a perforated edge, a registration mark, a rubber stamp, a ruled line. It appears at least three times.

**Section rhythm.** Sections are separated by 96px, with 128px after the hero. Inside a section, heading to body is 16px and body to action is 32px.

**Still no gradients and no glass.**

## Rules for the agent

- Write the headline before choosing a layout. If the headline could sit on a competitor's page, rewrite it.
- The hero contains a headline, one or two sentences, one primary action, and the product.
- Vary the sections: a wide image, a two-column comparison, a single quote, a table, a band of colour. Two sections in a row never share a layout.
- At least one section sits on a fill that is not the page colour.
- Body text is left-aligned. A headline may be centred only if it is under two lines.
- Every section answers a question a buyer has.
- Social proof uses real names and real numbers, or it is left out.
- One primary action per screen height. Repeat the same action down the page.
- Icons must carry meaning. A row of decorative icons above feature titles is removed.

## Look at it

Take a screenshot of the top of the page at desktop width and look at it for three seconds, the way a visitor would.

- Is there colour in it, apart from one button?
- Is the product the second thing you see, after the headline?
- Does it look like it was designed for this product, or for any product?
- Put it next to a plain white page with black text. Is yours clearly better, or only tidier?

If the answers are no, the page is not finished. Go back to the concept.
